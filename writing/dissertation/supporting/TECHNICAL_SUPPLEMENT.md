**How to read the two blocks.** This map exists because one result set is reported under several declared conventions, so the first question a reader has is which number is the result and where any other number's basis is fixed. Block A answers the first question by routing: it says where each claim's mechanics live and which main-text pointer each section answers, and it names the basis every section's artefacts sit on, so a count met later can be traced to a denominator without leaving the page. Block B answers the second by fixing nine families once. The primary object is the 69-cell combination grid on the seed-ensemble basis under volatility-unit QLIKE against the recalibrated HAR reference; the 180 standalone comparisons are a different object with a different denominator, a different reference and a different loss, and the two are never netted against each other. The 153-comparison subset is the 180 less the 27 price-arm rows, and it exists because the text and fusion verdict must be quoted on a denominator that contains no price arms. Where a family is corrected arm by arm rather than as a whole, the size of the arm's family and the size of the family it belongs to are both given, because a Holm p-value is only interpretable against the family it was corrected in: the anonymisation family is 12 statistic-bearing cells corrected as two families of 6, and the MAEC family is 24 corrected as three families of 8.

**What this map does not do.** It is a routing table, not a summary of findings: no cell states a verdict, an effect size or a significance, and the presence of a section here implies nothing about the strength of the evidence inside it. Block A's basis column names the convention an artefact sits on; it does not certify that the artefact's conclusion holds, and it does not rank sections. The pointer column names the main-text sentence a section services, not the only place a number appears; several of these claims are also stated in compressed form in the abstract and the introduction, and this column deliberately points at the section that carries the mechanics. Block B fixes the counting conventions; it does not report any count except the arithmetic that defines a family's size, and where it does quote a survivor count in its notes, that count is the one the main text states. Nothing in either block introduces a number that is not fixed elsewhere in the supplement or in the frozen main text.

### Figure S12. The forecaster-health screen reads the forecast distribution, not the label
![](figures/F12_health_screen_orthogonality.pdf)\

*The screen's two gate terms plotted against each other for the five cross-family probes and the primary-family anchor, with the increments the screen decides on drawn beneath the same axis and the 4K-truncated long-form rows in a separate band.*

Panel (a) is the screen's own plane. Its horizontal axis is the share of a run's forecasts that take the modal value after rounding to two decimals; its vertical axis is the standalone variance-unit (Patton-robust) QLIKE of those same forecasts, on a log scale. The registered formula calls a run healthy when the maximum over its three horizons of the modal share is below 60 per cent and the maximum of the variance-unit QLIKE is below 4, so the shaded quadrant is the pass region and the verdict attaches to a (family, channel) run rather than to a single point -- a marker to the left of the 60 per cent line can still belong to a run that breaches at another horizon. One marker is drawn per (family, channel, horizon) for the five cross-family probes (Llama-3.1-70B-AWQ, Yi-1.5-34B, Phi-4-14B, Mistral-Small-24B, Gemma-3-27B), with the primary Qwen3-32B family drawn under a distinct glyph as an anchor and excluded from every probe count, matching the frozen text's "five cross-family replications". The four-of-five failure count is taken per family and not per run: Yi-1.5-34B commits two channels and fails on both, so a per-run tally over the plotted markers would read five. Neither gate term reads the realised-volatility label and neither reads the increment: both are properties of the forecast distribution alone, which is what the main text means by calling the screen orthogonal to increment sign by construction.

Fill is a channel code and nothing else, on both panels: filled markers are 8-K (event-driven) runs and hollow markers are 10-K/Q (long-form) runs, as the panel (a) key states. Because the two panels share a horizontal axis and a reader carries that key downward, panel (b) keeps the same code and puts the health verdict entirely on a separate grey cross drawn over the failing markers, keyed inside the panel (b) axes rather than only in the line above them. The Gemma-3-27B validation pilot carries a glyph of its own, a filled plus with its own legend entry, so that it cannot be read as a Gemma long-form run: it is a 2,000-filing event-driven validation pass, and drawing it as a hollow copy of Gemma's diamond would have said the opposite under the fill code.

Panel (b) puts the quantity the screen decides on beneath the same horizontal axis. Its vertical axis is the volatility-unit relative QLIKE improvement over the recalibrated HAR -- the convention of the main table's cross-family block -- so the two panels carry two different QLIKE conventions and are labelled separately, because a number cannot be moved between them. The screen discards the largest positive increment in the whole block (Gemma-3-27B, +2.97 per cent at h=20) and retains a family whose increments run +0.70 to +1.39 per cent, which is the opposite of what a filter selecting for agreement would do. The screened-out families carry mixed signs, from Gemma's +1.84 / +2.59 / +2.97 to Yi's -0.62 at h=20 on 8-Ks and Phi-4's -0.12; those signs are printed so the mixture can be inspected, not so they can be counted, because a forecaster that emits one number for most documents is uninformative about the residual in either direction.

Three outcomes that run against the design are drawn rather than cropped. First, the anchor itself fails the screen off the 8-K channel: Qwen3-32B's long-form run reaches 64.4 and 76.8 per cent modal share at h=5 and h=10, which is why the surviving residual is scoped to 8-Ks in the main text, where the same family sits at 49--50 per cent. Second, Gemma-3-27B cleared the registered 2,000-filing validation pilot on both terms (45.2 per cent, 3.66) and then failed on the full 8-K test panel (71.54 per cent, 0.900 at h=5); the arrow between those two points spans two different splits and two different document counts, so it compares gate values and not effect sizes, and it moves down on variance-unit QLIKE while crossing the modal-share gate, so the full-panel failure is on modal share alone. Third, ranking the probes on QLIKE alone would invert the verdict, and the two QLIKE rankings name two different families: the lowest maximum-over-horizons variance-unit QLIKE among the five probes belongs to Phi-4-14B (0.677), while the lowest single cell over every committed probe row belongs to Gemma-3-27B (0.377 on its 3-run ensemble at h=20, 0.378 on the single-pass row panel (a) plots). Both are screened out on the modal-share term alone, so the two axes have to be read together. Table S11 carries the same two statistics with their variants named. Neither of them is Mistral-Small-24B: its lowest cell, 0.421, sits above both Gemma values.

The figure does not show significance. No p-value, Diebold--Mariano statistic or multiplicity correction appears on either panel; the per-probe inference, and the three different Holm conventions the source tables carry, are in Table S11. It also does not show every committed row. Panel (a) plots single-pass runs, because those are the rows carrying the complete health block: the 3-run ensembles of Mistral-Small-24B and Gemma-3-27B sit within 1.28 percentage points of modal share and 0.001 of variance-unit QLIKE of them, and the 70B's ensemble rows record no health columns at all, so they cannot be placed on the plane. Panel (b) instead plots the basis the frozen main table quotes, which for Gemma-3-27B is the 3-run ensemble. Yi-1.5-34B is quoted on its three context-clean 8-K rows; its three long-form rows carry the 4K-TRUNCATED flag against a roughly 6K-token excerpt budget, are drawn in a separate band on their own scale, and are not citable for the family claim, while its three combined rows carry PARTIAL(4K) and appear on neither panel. Finally, the 60 per cent and 4 thresholds are the registered ones, read at run time out of the formula the pilot record states verbatim rather than typed into the plotting script, and they were fixed before the Gemma probe ran; nothing in the figure estimates how the verdicts would move under a different threshold.

#### Footnotes

(i) **The Holm column mixes three conventions and must never be pooled.** The committed `holm_family` strings record Holm(3) *per reference* for Gemma-3-27B, Holm(6) *within its own family* for Mistral-Small-24B, and the committed Holm(6) *inherited* for the two anchors (Qwen3-32B and Llama-3.1-70B); Yi-1.5-34B and Phi-4-14B carry raw p only, with no correction columns in the source at all. A single "Holm p" column across these rows would not be a well-defined quantity.

(ii) **Ranking the probes on QLIKE alone would invert the verdict, and the two QLIKE rankings name two different families.** The lowest single-cell variance-unit QLIKE of any probe is Gemma-3-27B's 0.377 (3-run ensemble, h=20; its single-pass h=20 cell is 0.378), and Gemma too is screened out on the modal-share term alone: its maximum variance-unit QLIKE across both variants is 0.900 against a ceiling of 4, while its modal share runs 71.2--71.5%. Mistral-Small-24B runs 0.42--0.69 in variance-unit QLIKE at a modal share of 69.3--88.6%, above the 60% ceiling at every horizon and in both variants. On the gate's own maximum-over-horizons statistic the lowest of the five probes is Phi-4-14B at 0.677, also screened out on modal share alone. Both statistics are named because they identify different families, and neither is the statistic the modal-share term applies.

(iii) **Yi-1.5-34B's context flags.** Its three long-form rows carry `4K-TRUNCATED` and its three combined rows in `crossfamily_standalone.csv` carry `PARTIAL(4K)`, both against a roughly 6K-token excerpt budget; only its three event-driven rows are context-clean, and only those are citable for the family claim.

(iv) **The 70B ensemble rows carry no health columns.** `qlike_var`, `mode_share_pct`, `parse_fail_rate` and `clipped_rate` are all absent for `llama70_awq_ens3`, so that run cannot be screened.

(v) **The 70B ensemble is a reproducibility-jitter ensemble.** The seed is not plumbed into the sampler, so its three runs differ only through kernel non-determinism. Single versus ensemble moves the firm-reference estimate by at most 0.006pp (0.003 / 0.003 / 0.005 by horizon) while the best firm Holm p moves from 0.050012 to 0.048549 -- that is, the 70B's firm-rung significance is decided by kernel non-determinism.

(vi) **The larger point estimate carries the weaker DM.** The 70B's forecasts are about three times more dispersed than Qwen3-32B's (predictive s.d. 0.064--0.087 against 0.021--0.032, ratio 2.70--3.01) at identical n_test (25,109 / 25,001 / 24,732) and n_days (996 / 991 / 981), which inflates the loss-differential variance rather than shrinking the effect.

#### What the table shows and does not show

This is the per-probe record behind the cross-family paragraph: what each model was, on which channel it ran, how its forecast distribution behaved, what increment it produced, and under which correction its p-values were computed. Exactly one probe outside the primary family is instrument-healthy, and it replicates the residual's sign with a larger point estimate and weaker significance, which the table reports as a bound and not as a confirmation. The scope caveats are printed as table rows and footnotes rather than left in prose because each of them limits what a non-replication can mean: a truncated context window, a quantised build, an ensemble that could not vary its sampler, and three different multiplicity conventions inside one column. A non-replication produced by a forecaster that emits one number for most documents is not evidence against the residual, and a family-specificity claim is not identified at this sample of probes -- an earlier internal write-up asserted one and it was withdrawn; that headline is not reproduced here, and neither is the three-family verdict table in `crossfamily_mistral24.md`, which predates the Gemma-3-27B probe.

Moved here from the main text for space; the apparatus and the verdict are
unchanged.

MAEC (3,443 earnings-call transcripts) is the forecast-level counterpart to
the same-company critique of Yu et al. (2025). Running the identical ladder:

| stage | MAEC calls |
|---|---|
| apparent gain | 12/12 cells vs a raw baseline |
| + identity control (same-ticker mean) | 0/24 Holm (primary alignment); absorbs 89–133% of the gain (67–145% pooling both alignments) |
| zero-content probe | matches or exceeds the full transcript at 3 of 4 horizons |
| what survives | nothing above its MDE |
| 80%-power MDE | 3.0–12.8% |

**What the share is, and its scope.** The tabulated identity share is
`d4/d3 = (MSE_R − MSE_Re)/(MSE_R − MSE_U)`: the entity term's share of what
*text* adds over the *already-recalibrated* reference R. It is **not** a share
of the published-convention gain, whose denominator is the raw past-volatility
baseline; that comparison needs the separate converted quantity `G_conv`
(§6.4 of `maec_audit.md`), against which recalibration alone absorbs most of the
published gain and the entity term the remainder.

The main text quotes **89–133%**, the range over the primary alignment, which governs the prose ("primary (strict post-call
window) governs the paper wording"). Pooling both alignments widens it to
67–145%. Both ranges cover the two arms where the ratio is informative
(qwen embeddings, TF–IDF); the prompted-Qwen arm is excluded because its text
increment is essentially zero, so its ratio explodes to 2807–7890%,
uninformative rather than contradictory. A reader recomputing over all three
arms from `maec_audit.csv` will therefore get 67–7890%.

The `0 of 24` count is the primary alignment, three text arms × 4 horizons × 2
references, with Holm applied within each arm's family of 8. Of those 24, 18
have a positive published-convention gain to absorb, and 8 belong to the one
arm with a full published reading; the qwen-embedding arm's `G_conv` borrows
TF–IDF's Δ_pub as an upward-biased proxy (§7-5 of the same table), so its powered verdict is not
conservative.

So the published gain reproduces and then falls below detectability under
recalibration plus a same-ticker mean, and a probe carrying no transcript
content does as well as the transcript itself at three of four horizons, and the
same identity-shortcut pattern the SEC panel shows, in a corpus where the
published effect is an order of magnitude larger. The MDEs are wide, so this
panel bounds the published gain rather than resolving small effects.

### Figure S16. Portability: MAEC, reproduce then reprice
![](figures/F16_maec_reproduce_reprice.pdf)\

On a third public panel the audit first reproduces a published-style gain in full and then reprices it. Panel (a) is the reproduction. Under the published convention a fitted TF-IDF arm beats the raw past-volatility baseline at every horizon, by 53.1%, 38.0%, 32.8% and 13.6% of the raw pooled mean squared error, and it does so in 12 of the 12 panel-by-horizon cells. The prompted arm does not: it beats the raw baseline at the shortest horizon by 27.2% and loses at the other three by 28.6%, 50.0% and 72.4%, for 4 of 12 cells. The bars are sourced from the audit table's published-convention section rather than from the underlying prediction files, and they merge the published work's three annual panels row-equal-weight. That merge is a transport across panels that differ from this audit's own test window in both period and level, an approximation the source itself calls inherent to the conversion; the bars are descriptive and enter no correction.

Panel (b) is the repricing. With a recalibrated price forecast **and** a same-ticker expanding mean already in the reference, the residual is drawn for all 24 primary-alignment cells: three text arms, four horizons, two references. Each cell carries its date-block bootstrap interval as whiskers and a symmetric grey band at plus and minus that cell's own minimum detectable effect, which runs from 2.97% to 12.80%. Every one of the 24 residuals falls inside its own band, and 0 of 24 clear the Holm correction. That combination is why this panel is a bound rather than a demonstration of zero: it rules out a residual the size of the published gain and it does not rule out a small one. The point estimates are not all near zero either. The TF-IDF and frozen-embedding arms sit between +1.9% and +4.6%, which reads as a positive that the design cannot resolve; the prompted arm is negative at three of four horizons.

Two details are drawn rather than left in the source. Two cells are ringed because their bootstrap interval excludes zero while the Holm-corrected clustered test does not; both are the frozen-embedding arm at h=3, against the AR and the HAR reference respectively, at +4.27% (raw p .054, Holm .375) and +2.24% (raw p .027, Holm .220). The qualification matters: the second cell's clustered test does reject at the raw 5% level, and it is the 8-cell Holm correction that removes it, so this is a disagreement between the interval and the corrected test, not between the interval and an unrejected raw test. The correction governs, and neither cell enters a positive reading. Four cells are daggered because their within-date-swap gate is recorded unclean. That gate is a conjunction of two thresholds over five swap seeds, |mean swap DM| < 2.0 **and** mean p > .05, and a cell is unclean if it fails either: three of the four fail on p with a small mean DM (-0.17 at p .047, -0.84 at p .043, -1.91 at p .019) and one fails on magnitude (-2.05 at p .068). In each of the four the swapped combination is not cleanly separated from the reference, which points to a common call-date component rather than to document content; it does not mean the swapped combination beats the reference. Under the shifted alignment, which is not the governing one, the prompted arm's two h=15 cells are significantly **harmful** (Holm 0.0093 and 0.0433); the primary alignment's counterparts are merely negative.

**Basis, denominator and loss convention.** Panel (a) is a pooled mean squared error in squared-volatility units, transported from three published annual panels; panel (b) is a relative change in mean squared error expressed as a percentage of the reference's own MSE, where that reference already contains both the recalibrated price forecast and the same-ticker mean. Because the minimum detectable effect is defined on the same denominator and in the same unit, the band and the point are directly comparable, which is the only reason they may share an axis. Inference is call-date-clustered Diebold-Mariano with the Holm correction over the eight cells of each arm; the interval is a date-block bootstrap, a different device from the test, which is why the two can disagree on two cells. The panel is 3,443 calls, with 672 test rows, 143 call-date clusters and 461 entities per horizon, and 333 validation rows.

**What the figure does not show, and the scope limits.** Three limits belong here. First, the identity share tabulated in the source, which this figure does not plot, is the entity term's share of what text adds over an **already recalibrated** reference; it is not a share of the published-convention gain in panel (a), and the two must not be read as the same quantity. Second, the frozen-embedding arm has no published reading of its own, so its power verdict borrows the TF-IDF arm's published gain as a proxy; that gain is the largest of the three arms, so the proxy is biased upward and the resulting powered reading is not conservative for that arm. Third, panel (a)'s quantities are transported across panels and periods, so they establish that the published-style comparison reproduces on this corpus, not that the numbers are directly comparable with the published ones. Nothing on this figure is a QLIKE quantity, nothing shares a denominator with the SEC panel, and no cell in this table qualifies for a positive reading in either direction.

**What the table shows.** Pairwise tests control error only across the pairs actually run, which leaves open the objection that a null was assembled from a post-hoc choice of comparisons. T4 replaces the pairwise family with joint tests over the entire alternative set. Each row is one loss-panel: a disclosure channel crossed with a horizon and a loss, evaluated on that panel's common inner-joined sample, with multi-seed neural models seed-ensembled, losses aggregated to one value per model per trading day so the day dimension matches the day-clustered primary, and a stationary bootstrap over days at expected block length equal to the horizon. K is the number of models on that panel -- the HAR-RV benchmark plus its 25 alternatives, or 26 alternatives on event-driven, with the alternative-set size printed in brackets -- and the pool column splits K into price, text and fusion arms. The benchmark is one of the 8 price arms and is itself a candidate for the confidence set, which is why it can be excluded from that set in 8 of 18 panels while never being one of its own alternatives in the SPA test.

Two columns carry the result. The text/fusion SPA p, which tests "no text or fusion model beats HAR" against that block alone, is 1.000 in all 18 panels; the best member of the block is D1_concat_mlp or D3_gteqwen2 and its t is negative in every one of the 18, ranging from -4.09 to -0.51, so the best-looking text or fusion alternative is worse than the benchmark rather than marginally better. The MCS90 text column is 0 in all 18 panels: across the 222 text-model rows in the per-model file, no block-B dictionary or bag-of-words arm, no block-C encoder, and neither prompted-LLM arm is ever admitted to the 90% model confidence set. The full-set SPA p, which admits the price models as alternatives, falls below .05 in 9 of the 18 panels, and in each of those nine the best challenger is a price model: A6_shar in four, A6_harq in three, A7_harx_vix in two. The same asymmetry shows from the other direction in the standalone strip: under the variance-unit QLIKE convention, 7 of the 180 standalone comparisons do favour the challenger, and all 7 are price models (A3 GARCH six times, A4 EGARCH once), a count scoped to that unit convention and to price challengers and not comparable with the squared-error 0 of 180 or with the 0 of 153 text and fusion comparisons.

The fusion admission is printed rather than summarised. Fusion arms enter the 90% set in 12 of the 18 panels, 24 slots in total, against 73 price slots and 0 text slots, and the arms responsible are named above. This is what one expects of models that carry the HAR price signal inside them: they can fail to be separated from the best model without ever beating it, and the SPA test never rejects in their favour. Reading a fusion slot as a text result would be an error the table is built to pre-empt. The price column is printed for the same reason and is not uniformly favourable to the paper's benchmark: the A2 HAR-RV baseline is itself outside the 90% set in 8 of 18 panels, all of them event-driven or combined, which is the joint-test counterpart of the main text's observation that HAR-RV is not the QLIKE-strongest price model and the reason the reference interval is closed by a maximal price pool rather than by HAR alone.

**What the table does not show.** It measures separation, not magnitude. A model can sit inside the confidence set while adding nothing, and confidence-set membership is not evidence of incremental value against a recalibrated reference. Nor does the table test incremental value at all: the alternatives here are standalone forecasts, so nothing in these 18 rows speaks to whether text adds on top of a price baseline, which is what the reference ladder measures on a separate 69-cell grid. It reports no power: a non-rejecting SPA carries no minimum detectable effect, and the injected-signal calibration that accompanies the paper's null counts is not run in this design. The 18 panels are not 18 independent experiments. The combined channel is the exact union of the other two, verified row by row (31,405 = 7,550 + 23,855 at h = 5, and likewise at h = 10 and h = 20), the two losses are computed on the same forecasts, and no multiplicity correction is applied across the 18 rows, so the 9 rejections should not be read as 9 independent events. The row basis is also narrower than a single-arm evaluation: n_obs is each panel's common inner-joined sample across all K models, which on event-driven is 23,855 / 22,785 / 22,318 rows at h = 5/10/20 rather than the 25,109 / 25,001 / 24,732 rows of the full event-driven panel that single-arm tables evaluate on, so no count here may be set against a count computed on the full panel. Finally, the prompted-LLM arms appear here only as standalone alternatives; the bounded 8-K residual the paper reports is an increment over a firm-identity reference, a quantity this table does not evaluate, and its absence from every model confidence set is a statement about standalone skill alone.

**Footnotes.**

(i) *What membership means.* Model-confidence-set membership means "cannot be statistically separated from the best model at the 10% size", not "beats HAR". Fusion models enter in 12 of 18 panels for 24 slots in total; that is the number to quote, and it is not a text result.

(ii) *Loss units.* The mean losses in the source file are computed on each panel's common inner-joined sample, QLIKE in volatility units and squared error in volatility-squared units. They are not on the same scale as the variance-unit QLIKE levels the standalone leaderboard reports in S7, and the protocol states that levels are never compared across the two unit conventions. No loss level is printed in this table.

(iii) *Which SPA p.* Every SPA column is Hansen's consistent p. The source file also carries the lower and upper variants; they are deliberately not reported here, because the consistent p is the one the main text quotes and printing all three invites selective reading. The nine daggers mark the panels where the full-set consistent p is below .05.

(iv) *Cross-check.* The independent `arch` implementation was run alongside the transparent numpy engine and its note is "ok" in all 18 rows.

(v) *K, the alternative set, and why they differ by channel.* K is the model count including the benchmark, which is what the source file records as `n_models`; the SPA alternative set is K - 1, the value in brackets, because the benchmark is excluded from the set it is tested against. The pool is 8 price, 12 text and 6 fusion arms on long-form and combined, giving K = 26 and 25 alternatives, and 8 / 13 / 6 on event-driven, giving K = 27 and 26 alternatives, where one extra prompted-LLM text arm (the 70B cross-family probe) is carried. The extra arm is a text model, so it widens the set the text column is drawn from without changing the fact that the column is 0. The generator aborts unless exactly one row per panel is flagged as the benchmark, that row is A2_har_rv, its block is price, the per-panel model rows equal `n_models`, and the alternative-set sizes are exactly {25, 26}.

### Figure S4. The reference ladder, cell by cell
![](figures/F4_ladder_cell_matrix.pdf)\

*Every cell of the 69-cell combination grid against five rungs of the reference ladder (seed-ensemble basis, vol-unit QLIKE), drawn as two panels: event-driven (24 rows) on the left, long-form (45 rows) on the right, one row per (model, horizon). A filled square marks survival at that rung. Whole-grid counts, printed once in the key: 38 primary, 9 maximal-pool, 8 firm-identity, 0 for the pool-and-identity conjunction, 0 for the full conjunction. Per-panel subtotals above each matrix: 8 / 0 / 6 / 0 / 0 of the 24 event-driven cells and 30 / 9 / 2 / 0 / 0 of the 45 long-form cells. Right margin of each panel: the primary-rung relative QLIKE improvement per cell, on one scale shared by both panels, spanning -3.86% to +5.92%.*

The headline ladder is normally read as four counts. This figure reads it as 345 decisions. Each row is one grid cell, that is one (channel, model, horizon) triple; each column is one rung; a filled square means the cell survives that rung. The 69 rows are split into two side-by-side panels purely so that every label clears 9 pt at the size the figure is included: nothing is subsetted, the two panels together are the whole grid, the bar scale is common to both, and the counts in the key are for all 69 cells. The basis throughout is the seed-ensemble primary: vol-unit QLIKE, combiner weights fitted on validation and frozen on test, day-clustered Diebold-Mariano inference, Holm correction inside the 69-cell family, and, for column (1) only, the additional requirement that the label-shuffle placebo returns |DM| < 2. The denominator is 69 in every column of the key, so the counts 38, 9, 8, 0, 0 are directly comparable with one another; the per-panel subtotals printed above each matrix have denominators 24 and 45 and are the decomposition of those same five counts (8+30, 0+9, 6+2, 0+0, 0+0).

Two properties of the ladder are visible here and are not visible in any aggregate. First, columns (4) and (5) contain no filled square in any of the 69 rows, in either panel: the conjunction of the maximal-pool rung and the firm-identity rung is empty, and so is the full conjunction that also demands the primary rung. Second, no row anywhere is filled in both column (2) and column (3). The 9 cells that survive the five-model price pool and the 8 that survive the firm-identity reference are disjoint sets, not nested ones, which is why their conjunction is 0 rather than min(9, 8) = 8. The two-panel view also prices that observation honestly, and the artefact says so: all 9 pool survivors are long-form and column (2) is empty in the event-driven panel, so disjointness in that panel is forced by an empty column rather than earned. The informative case is the long-form panel, where 9 pool survivors and 2 identity survivors coexist in the same 45 rows and still do not intersect. A reader who assumed nesting would read the empty conjunction as arithmetically forced everywhere; in the long-form panel it is not.

The margin bars are there to prevent the opposite error, that the surviving primary-rung increments are uniformly negligible. They are not. The largest reaches +5.92% relative QLIKE improvement against the recalibrated HAR (long-form, B2_tfidf_ridge, h=20), and the most negative cell reaches -3.86% (long-form, C2_finbert_s3, h=20); both extremes are labelled inside their own bar. The two panels share one bar scale so cells can be compared across channels, and negative cells are drawn rather than clipped. It is precisely because some primary-rung increments are large that the stronger references are needed to adjudicate them.

What the figure does not show. It does not show effect sizes at the stronger rungs: the bars are the primary rung only, so a filled square in column (2) or (3) carries no size information here (the pool-rung and identity-rung magnitudes are tabulated separately). It does not show raw-p survival, only Holm survival, so it understates how many cells clear an uncorrected threshold (raw counts are 46 / 26 / 15 / 4 / 4 in the same file). It does not show the placebo outcome for columns (2) to (5); only column (1) is placebo-gated. It says nothing about power: the emptiness of columns (4) and (5) is a property of a strict selection device applied to this panel, not a measurement that the underlying effect is zero, and what that device is powered to detect is quantified separately by the injected-signal calibration. And the left-right arrangement carries no information: the event-driven panel is drawn first because it is smaller, not because it is prior in any ordering.

Scope limit and basis. This matrix is drawn from `control_intersection_ensemble.csv` only. The superseded single-seed file `control_intersection.csv` gives 29 / 8 / 8 on the same three rungs and is reported in the basis section, never mixed into this matrix. The seed glyph marks which cells are affected by that distinction at all: 36 cells are single-seed on both bases (18 event-driven, 18 long-form) and are drawn with an open circle; only the 33 cells drawn with a filled circle are three-seed ensembles.

### Figure S5. Is the maximal pool a shopped reference?
![](figures/F5_maximal_pool_audit.pdf)\

*Four audits of the five-model price pool. (a) Holm survivors of 69 by reference specification, ordered by survivors left, seed-ensemble bars with seed-2026 ghost bars and the 38-cell primary rung as a dashed rule. (b) Test QLIKE for four price references, one axis per disclosure-by-horizon panel, with the long-form h=20 panel marked. (c) The same 15 cells against each of the five single price references and against the fitted pool, seed-2026 basis, outlined where clustered p < .05. (d) HAR against semivariance HAR on 18 identical cells, drawn as two 9-row blocks on one shared scale. Completing the price frontier with HARQ and HAR-X gives mean test QLIKE 0.0887 for the HAR-X pool against 0.0916 for the paper's five-model pool, better in 4 of 6 panels, and cuts Holm survivors from 8 to 3 within the seed-2026 basis.*

The charge this figure answers has two halves: that the reference was chosen to minimise text's credit, and that the pool wins only because six weights were fitted on validation. Panel (c) answers the first half directly and unfavourably to any single-reference study, including a hypothetical version of this one. On one cell -- long-form, B2_tfidf_ridge, h=5, marked in the row labels -- the reported increment is +3.39% against HAR, +3.53% against semivariance HAR, +1.66% against GARCH, +1.06% against EGARCH and +3.24% against ARIMA. The same forecasts, the same rows, the same loss: a range of 2.5 percentage points produced entirely by the choice of price model. HAR is not the reference that flatters text least, either; on the committed labels HAR is the best of the five single price references in 0 of 6 disclosure-by-horizon panels. This is why the pool exists, and why "against a single recalibrated HAR" is the load-bearing qualifier in the paper's own primary rung.

Panels (a) and (b) answer the second half in both directions. Panel (a) counts Holm survivors out of 69 under each reference specification: the validation-best single member leaves 34, the never-fitted equal-weight five-model pool leaves 17, and the fitted five-model pool leaves 9, against 38 for the single recalibrated HAR that defines the primary rung. Because the equal-weight pool fits nothing beyond the two recalibration coefficients every reference gets, most of the absorption cannot be weight fitting: an unfitted pool already cuts the primary rung from 38 to 17 (19 on the seed-2026 basis, shown as ghost bars). The ordering in (a) is by absorption, not by test loss, and the panel title says so, because the two orderings disagree: on test QLIKE the never-fitted equal-weight pool beats the fitted pool in 6 of 6 panels, so a reference that leaves fewer survivors is not thereby the better forecaster.

Panel (b) shows the cost of that argument in full. Each row is one panel with its own QLIKE axis, oriented so that lower loss is to the left, and the numeric loss at each end of the axis is printed so the row can be decoded without a shared scale. The fitted pool beats the member a forecaster could actually have selected on validation in 5 of the 6 panels (clustered DM -7.24 to -2.05), which is the answer to the overfitting charge as usually stated. In the sixth, long-form h=20, the reverse holds and the panel is marked: the fitted pool is significantly worse than the validation-best member there (DM +8.49, p < 1e-15) and at 0.0731 it is the worst of the four references drawn, behind the validation-best member (0.0714), the test-best member (0.0689) and the equal-weight pool (0.0680). Across all six panels the fitted pool loses to the never-fitted equal-weight pool, which is the strongest available objection to the reported rung and is drawn here rather than described. The test-best member is included and labelled as a hindsight selection: it is not available without reading the test rows, and comparisons against it are not evidence about a feasible forecaster.

Panel (d) removes the remaining escape route, that the collapse is an artefact of a weak single price reference. Swapping the recalibrated HAR for a recalibrated semivariance HAR on 18 identical cells moves the increments almost nowhere: the largest shift anywhere in the panel is 0.18 percentage points (long-form, C5_qwen3, h=10), and in every one of the 18 rows the semivariance diamond sits inside the HAR ring drawn around it.

The caption's frontier audit is a self-criticism and belongs in the record. Completing the price frontier with HARQ and HAR-X gives a mean test QLIKE of 0.0887 for the HAR-X pool against 0.0916 for the pool this paper uses, better in 4 of 6 panels, and cuts Holm survivors from 8 to 3. HAR-X is therefore a better pool member than several that were included, and was left out because the pool composition was fixed before that audit was run. Completing the frontier strengthens the null rather than weakening it. The 8-to-3 movement is a within-basis delta on the seed-2026 basis and is claimable only as such; it is never a restatement of the committed 9-of-69 count, which is on the seed-ensemble basis.

What the figure does not show, and scope. Panel (a) counts Holm survivors, not effect sizes, so a specification that leaves fewer survivors is not thereby shown to leave smaller increments, and, as (b) establishes, it is not thereby the better forecaster either. Panel (b) reports pooled panel-level test QLIKE and says nothing about which grid cells move; its within-row positions are normalised between that row's own minimum and maximum, so distances are readable within a row and not between rows. Panel (c) covers only the 15 cells of the committed single-reference sweep -- three long-form and two event-driven challengers at three horizons -- not the full 69-cell grid, and it is drawn entirely on the seed-2026 text basis, including its fitted-pool column, because the single-reference sweep is written on that basis and mixing the ensemble pool column in would put 6 of the 15 rows on a different basis from their own neighbours. Panel (d) carries neither Holm correction nor a placebo and its Diebold-Mariano statistics in the source are observation-level rather than day-clustered, so it is a magnitude comparison and nothing more. The frontier audit in the caption is likewise seed-2026 only.

### Table S7. Which firm, not when: the within-date decomposition
Two permutations separate what a surviving increment is made of. A within-date permutation shuffles the text forecast among the filings that share a trading day: every date, every regime and every daily cross-sectional mean is preserved, and only which-firm correspondence is destroyed. A date-mean-text combiner replaces each filing's text forecast with that day's mean, which is a pure when-signal carrying no cross-sectional content at all. If an increment is a calendar effect, the first permutation leaves it intact and the second reproduces it; if it is cross-sectional, the first destroys it and the second delivers nothing.

Classification rule, verbatim from the source: regime if date-mean share >= 0.5 or within-date placebo survival >= 0.5; cross-sectional if both <= 0.25. Cells satisfying neither condition are mixed. The date-mean share is the ratio of the date-mean combiner's relative improvement to the real relative improvement, clipped to [0, 1]; the cross-sectional share is one minus it. All Diebold-Mariano statistics behind the cross-sectional / regime-timing verdicts are day-clustered, and the real relative improvements reproduce the committed single-seed grid to a maximum absolute difference of 0.0 in all 69 cells.

The median genuine cell keeps none of its increment when firm correspondence is destroyed, and a pure timing signal delivers none of it. That is what distinguishes this finding from aggregate sentiment or implied-volatility tracking, where the signal would be a calendar effect that any within-date permutation leaves untouched.

#### (c) Basis

This decomposition is computed on the single-seed 2026 basis, and only on it. Its real relative improvements reproduce the committed single-seed grid exactly, cell for cell, including the 33 cells whose seed-ensemble value differs.

The 38-cell membership is inherited from the original combination grid, whose selection used observation-level HAC Diebold-Mariano statistics -- the superseded convention; the source's own RESTATED-vs-ORIGINAL table records it as roughly twice inflated. Only the within-date and date-mean statistics reported here are day-clustered. On the single-seed day-clustered basis 29 of 69 cells are genuine, and that set agrees with this table's 38-cell set in 54 of 69 cells: 12 cells classified here are not clustered-genuine on that basis, and 3 clustered-genuine cells are not classified here.

Joined instead to the seed-ensemble primary on (channel, model, horizon), the two tables' genuine flags differ in 16 of the 69 cells, 8 in each direction; both flags are 38-true, so the two 38-cell sets are the same size but they are not the same 38 cells. (The complementary agreement count, 53 of 69, is a cell-set agreement count and has nothing to do with the firm-identity comparison, whose own 53-of-69 row count is a repeated per-panel value that collapses to 4 of 6 distinct channel-by-horizon comparisons.) The 33 / 4 / 1 split is therefore a property of this table's own basis and its own cell set. Its rows must not be cross-joined onto an ensemble-basis cell list, and the split must not be quoted beside an ensemble-basis figure without the single-seed tag.

#### What this section does not show

The table classifies the character of increments that exist at the primary rung. It says nothing about whether any of those increments survive the maximal-pool or firm-identity references; a cell can be cleanly cross-sectional here and absorbed entirely at the next rung, and many are. The two permutations are also not a complete decomposition: a cell can fail to be reproduced by a date-mean signal and still be driven by something other than firm-specific document content, which is what the firm-identity reference and the anonymisation arm are for. The classification thresholds (0.5 and 0.25) are the committed rule and are not varied here, so cells near a boundary -- long-form B3_lm_linear at h=10, with a regime share of 0.507 against a 0.5 cut -- would change verdict under a slightly different rule. Finally, the date-mean column is not near zero outside those five cells: 28 of the 33 cross-sectional cells carry a negative date-mean value, the most negative among them being long-form C2_finbert_s4 at h=20 at -15.34%, meaning a pure timing signal there actively damages the forecast rather than merely failing to help. Larger negative date-mean values still, down to -43.61% at long-form B4_lm_features h=20, occur among the 31 cells that are not genuine and are therefore unclassified.

## S14. Power, minimum detectable effects, and what the null does not rule out

### Figure S9. What the null is powered to see: injected-signal recovery, per-cell minimum detectable effects, and the label's own noise
![](figures/F9_power_mde_label_noise.pdf)\

*Figure S9. (a) Cells detected out of 69 at each rung of the reference ladder, on real data and at three injected effect sizes, with the sixteen counts carried in the key beneath the panel. (b) Each cell's real relative QLIKE improvement over the recalibrated HAR reference against that cell's own 80%-power minimum detectable effect. Panel (a) measures what the reference ladder can find. The device is re-run four times on the 69-cell primary family: once on the real data, and three times with an oracle firm-orthogonal signal injected into the text forecast and calibrated per cell so that the realised test relative QLIKE improvement of the combined forecast over the reference is exactly 0.3%, 0.5% and 1.0% in every cell simultaneously. On the real data the recalibrated-HAR rung detects 38 of 69, the firm-identity rung 8, the maximal-pool rung 9, and the conjunction of all three 0. With signal injected at 0.3% the conjunction recovers 2 of 69, at 0.5% it recovers 6, and at 1.0% it recovers 13; the individual rungs recover 12 / 20 / 41 (HAR), 7 / 11 / 20 (firm identity) and 6 / 12 / 19 (maximal pool). The 0 of 69 on real data is therefore a property of a strict selection device with measured sensitivity, not a demonstration that nothing is there.

The counts are read from the key rather than from the plotting area. Over most of the injected range the rungs run within one cell of each other -- 7 against 6 at 0.3%, 11 against 12 at 0.5%, 20 against 19 at 1.0% -- which is about two points of vertical separation at this panel size, so no label printed inside the axes could be bound to its own series by position. All sixteen counts are therefore printed once, in the key beneath the panel, one row per rung, in the column order of the x axis (real, then 0.3, 0.5 and 1.0), with each row carrying its own marker and colour. The curves in the panel carry the shape of the recovery; the key carries the numbers.

The basis is the seed-ensemble primary family: 15 long-form and 8 event-driven challenger arms crossed with horizons 5, 10 and 20, per-observation seed means for the three-seed arms, a single recalibrated HAR as the reference, and a log-space combiner fitted on validation and frozen on test. The loss is volatility-unit QLIKE throughout, and a cell counts as detected when the day-clustered Diebold--Mariano statistic is negative and the Holm-adjusted p-value is below .05 within that (rung, level) family of 69. At the injected levels the placebo gate is not part of the detection criterion; at the real-data column the resulting counts 38 / 8 / 9 reproduce the committed ensemble ladder counts exactly. The firm-identity and maximal-pool rungs are evaluated on the five-price inner-join panel, whose test rows are a subset of the HAR-rung panel, so the three rungs are not scored on identical row sets.

The annotation beside panel (a) carries the construction's one dangerous property. Because the injection equalises the realised effect at exactly the target level, cells whose real effect already exceeded that level have signal removed rather than added: at 0.3 / 0.5 / 1.0% the calibrated coefficient is negative in 50 / 47 / 42 of the 69 cells. Each curve is a recovery rate at one fixed effect size, not a comparison of effect sizes, and nothing in it implies that the real effects exceed 1%. It is also why the HAR rung falls from 38 to 12 when 0.3% is injected. The whole panel is an oracle construction built from test labels, the single declared exception to the no-look-ahead rule; it calibrates sensitivity and is never citable as forecasting performance. All 207 (cell, level) calibrations converged to within 0.02pp of their target.

Panel (b) is the unfavourable half and is drawn without cropping. Each of the 69 cells is plotted at its own 80%-power, 5%-size minimum detectable effect against its real, no-injection relative QLIKE improvement over the recalibrated HAR reference. The minimum detectable effect has a median of 0.82% with an interquartile range of [0.37, 1.27] and a full span of 0.012% to 3.647%; the medians by channel and horizon run 1.07 / 0.84 / 1.27% for long-form at h = 5 / 10 / 20 and 0.44 / 0.46 / 0.96% for event-driven, so the ranges the main text quotes (0.84--1.27% long-form, 0.44% event-driven at h = 5) are medians of those subgroups rather than of the pooled 69. The real effects span -3.86% to +5.92%. Forty of the 69 cells sit at or above their own threshold on the signed comparison and 29 sit below it, which means that for a substantial part of the grid an economically small true effect would have been invisible whichever way the test came out. Four of the 38 cells the HAR rung detects lie below their own prospective threshold; that is expected, since the minimum detectable effect is an 80%-power planning quantity computed from a day-clustered HAC standard error and not a significance bound. Negative effects are plotted where they fall and are not clipped, and no shaded band is drawn behind the points, because a band on this chart would read as a confidence interval it is not.

Panel (c) explains why those thresholds are as wide as they are, without using the explanation as an excuse. Two nearly unbiased estimators of the same latent integrated variance over the same window -- the close-to-close label itself and a Garman--Klass estimator built from the same daily bars -- identify the label's own estimator noise without any high-frequency data. On non-overlapping test-era windows (100,485 at h = 5, 49,748 at h = 10, 24,617 at h = 20) the noise share of the log close-to-close label is 54.8%, 43.8% and 34.9%, which caps the R-squared attainable against this label at 0.452, 0.562 and 0.651 even with perfect knowledge of the latent variance. About half of the five-day label's variance is the estimator's own error.

What panel (c) does not show is as important as what it does. The identity prices the intraday component only: roughly 34% of the close-to-close label's variance is the overnight return, which the Garman--Klass comparison cannot see and which is not identified here, so the decomposition is a bounded partial accounting rather than a complete noise budget. The two estimator errors are also positively correlated, both reading the same price path, which makes each tabulated share a lower bound on the intraday component. Nothing in this panel is a forecasting result, and the R-squared ceiling is a property of the label, not of any model in the benchmark. Finally, the whole figure is scoped to volatility-unit QLIKE on the seed-ensemble primary family; the variance-unit convention and the label-perturbation families are treated separately.

What the figure does not show should be read alongside what it does. **Explanation.** The paper's mechanism claim is that a weak reference combined with permissive inference manufactures apparent text value. This table demonstrates that rather than asserting it by analogy: it runs a published-style evaluation design on this panel and then repairs one component at a time, so that each movement can be attributed to the single change that produced it. The design's in-sample arm reproduces the published-style positive at full strength -- an R-squared lift from 0.250 to 0.381 at h=5, from 0.312 to 0.455 at h=10 and from 0.336 to 0.495 at h=20, at MSE reductions of +17.4 to +23.9% and naive t statistics between +42.9 and +47.4 -- while its year-by-year out-of-sample arm on the same panel yields -0.68%, -4.85% and -7.65%. The repairs then move the verdict in both directions, which is the point of reading the rungs in order rather than a single before-and-after comparison. Recalibrating the reference at L2 makes text look *better*, turning -3.16% into +3.48% at h=10: on this panel the weak single-control baseline was itself so miscalibrated that text was being charged for the control's error. Switching to day-clustered inference at L3 leaves the gains identical and only shrinks the statistic, from DM -15.30 to -8.89 at h=10. Adding the firm-identity term at L4 flips all three horizons significantly negative, and adding the five-price pool at L5 leaves two of the three negative -- but not the third.

The exception is stated rather than summarised over. At the strictest rung the h=20 cell reverses to +0.20% with day-clustered DM -2.85 and Holm .0045, which the source records as 'text adds', while h=5 and h=10 stay significantly negative at Holm 7.12e-04 and 1.95e-06. The reading is that adding the price pool changes the reference's error profile at the longest horizon, on a row support that shrinks from 7,902 to 7,097 as the inner join over all five price models bites; it is a cell-level residual in a three-test family, not a cell of the protocol grid, and the protocol additionally imposes the placebo gate and 69-cell family Holm, under which this model and channel does not survive the firm-identity control.

**What this table does not show.** It is not a replication of any original study's findings: it does not re-evaluate that study's corpus, period or 12-month horizon, and a cross-sectional detection result -- which firm is riskier -- is untouched by it, because the dissolve concerns incremental time-series forecast value. It uses one text model, so it says nothing about whether other representations would traverse the ladder the same way; the 69-cell grid elsewhere in the supplement is where that question is answered. Its L0 and L1 rungs are scored in log-volatility MSE and its L2-L5 rungs in volatility-unit QLIKE, so the text-gain column is not comparable in level across the L1/L2 boundary; a continuity check on the same forecasts in QLIKE is available in the source table. Its in-sample arm is a deliberate look-ahead and must never be quoted as a result of this benchmark. Finally, two vocabulary conventions must not be conflated. For every out-of-sample rung the TF-IDF vocabulary and inverse document frequency are fit on the 2010-2019 training filings only and then frozen, which the source records as leakage-free for every out-of-sample year: that is a leakage control, not a concession, and it is not the reason any out-of-sample rung reads as it does. The in-sample arm instead refits the inverse document frequency and the ridge on all rows while keeping the vocabulary train-only, and it is that single restriction which the source calls a conservative simplification that can only weaken L0's positive -- so the manufactured effect reported in block (c) is a lower bound on what an unrestricted in-sample design would produce, and no such bound is claimed for the ladder rows in block (b).

## S17. Economic adjudication

### Figure S14. Economic adjudication: does the residual pay?
![](figures/F14_economic_adjudication.pdf)\

*Figure S14. (a) Change in annualised Sharpe from replacing the recalibrated-HAR volatility forecast with the text-augmented one in a long-only inverse-variance book, all 18 grid cells, day-block bootstrap whiskers. (b) Realised left-tail violation rates for the raw HAR, the recalibrated HAR and the text-augmented forecast, 36 rows at each of two nominal levels, pooled-drift mode; the ringed marker is the single cell of 108 that under-violates at the nominal 1% level.*

Statistical survival and economic value are different claims, so the surviving increment is priced in two decision problems that do not share a convention. Panel (a) is a portfolio problem: each rebalance day the live filing signals are weighted in inverse proportion to their forecast variance, the book is held for non-overlapping h-day periods, and the quantity plotted is the change in annualised Sharpe when the text-augmented forecast replaces the recalibrated-HAR one, with whiskers from a day-block bootstrap over blocks of h periods. The interval covers zero in 14 of the 15 cells that have one, including 5 of the prompted arm's 6; the grid median is +0.003. The one cell whose interval excludes zero is the event-driven TF-IDF ridge at h=10, +0.0257 with interval [+0.0036, +0.0474], which is about what a grid of eighteen intervals produces by chance. Three cells are drawn as bare dots because their interval is undefined: the long-form h=20 book has only 39 holding periods and the bootstrap uses 20-period blocks. An undefined interval covers nothing, which is why the coverage count is taken over the 15 cells that have one rather than over all 18. Those three deserve a second sentence rather than a footnote, because their reference book loses money (Sharpe -0.104), so a positive Sharpe difference there improves a losing position and is not evidence that the text forecast is profitable.

Panel (b) prices the two moves separately. It plots realised violation rates, not comparisons: for each of 36 (disclosure, model, horizon) rows at each of two nominal levels, the raw HAR, the recalibrated HAR and the text-augmented forecast are drawn as three markers against a bold rule at the nominal level. The distance from the raw-HAR square to the blue circle is what recalibration buys; the distance from the blue circle to the vermillion triangle is what adding text buys. The two are counted rather than eyeballed, because at this scale the triangle covers the circle in most rows: the recalibration move is the larger of the two in 69 of 72 cells, with medians of 0.95 against 0.05 percentage points. The reference-against-itself rows in the source file, which carry a Diebold-Mariano statistic of exactly 0 with p = 1 by construction, are excluded from every tally rather than drawn as ties. The panel deliberately keeps the recalibrated reference's own miscalibration in frame: at the 1% level 107 of the 108 forecast-cells over-violate, and the single exception -- the long-form Qwen3 embedding at h=20 under the text-augmented forecast, at 0.99% -- is ringed on the artefact and named there, so the exception is visible and not merely counted. That cell sits 0.014 of a percentage point below nominal, close enough to the rule that widening the axis cannot separate them, which is why it is ringed rather than left to position alone.

The tick-loss tally is the most cherry-pickable number in this section, so it ships with its complement. Against the recalibrated reference, text lowers the Gonzalez-Rivera tick loss in 55 of 72 cells, significantly in 29, and is significantly worse in 12; it moves the violation rate closer to nominal in 35 of 72, which is no better than a coin. Recalibration alone improves the tick loss in 60 of 72 cells, 54 of them significantly. The utility-fee table agrees in the same direction and is not redrawn here.

**Basis, denominator and loss convention.** Panel (a) is an annualised Sharpe ratio computed by scaling non-overlapping h-day log returns by the square root of 252/h; it is a difference of two Sharpe ratios, not a loss ratio, and its units have nothing to do with the ladder's relative-QLIKE percentages. Its denominator is the number of holding periods, which falls from 200 at event-driven h=5 to 39 at long-form h=20, and that fall is why three intervals are undefined. Panel (b) is a realised violation rate, a proportion of filing rows whose h-day forward return fell below a Gaussian left-tail value-at-risk built from the forecast volatility; the pooled-drift mode is the only one drawn, and the 72 cells are 36 (disclosure, model, horizon) rows at each of two nominal levels. Neither panel is a QLIKE quantity and neither may be placed on an axis with any ladder number.

**What the figure does not show, and the scope limits.** Two limits matter and neither is cosmetic. First, the value-at-risk and utility tables contain no rows at all for the prompted arm, so panel (b) corroborates the grid but does not adjudicate the surviving 8-K residual; only panel (a) touches that arm, and it prices it at zero of six cells with an interval excluding zero. Second, the Christoffersen conditional-coverage statistics available in the source are indicative only, because h-day return windows from filings less than h trading days apart overlap and induce serial dependence the independence test does not model; the unconditional coverage test and the tick loss are unaffected, and the Diebold-Mariano comparisons use a heteroskedasticity- and autocorrelation-consistent lag of h-1. The figure also does not show transaction costs, borrowing constraints or a short side: the book is long-only and frictionless, so the Sharpe differences are an upper bound on what an implementable version would deliver. Nor does panel (b) show a test: it shows levels, and the three markers on a row are three forecasts against one nominal rule, not three pairwise comparisons.

## S18. Portability: the Yelp panel

### Figure S15. Portability: the Yelp panel, where the identity control is itself costly
![](figures/F15_yelp_portability.pdf)\

*Figure S15. Panel (b) shows why the surviving rung is credited to content and how far that credit extends. Two rungs are marked rather than claimed. **Basis, denominator and loss convention.** The loss is squared error on stars, so mean squared error rather than QLIKE; inference is month-clustered Diebold-Mariano with a business x month two-way robustness variant; every percentage is a relative change in MSE against the reference named beside it, so the denominator changes across rungs. **What the figure does not show, and the scope limits.** Nothing here shares a denominator, a loss or a panel with the SEC results, and no number on this figure may be placed on an axis with a QLIKE percentage. The machinery-validation fixture on an injected data-generating process is a separate object and is not a Yelp result; it is not drawn here. The figure does not show a residual robust to every diagnostic: the three-month content rung fails its swap gate, and the panel makes that visible with a hatch instead of dropping the bar. It does not establish that the identity control is costly in general; it establishes that its sign and size are properties of the panel and its baseline, which is the whole point of running the same apparatus twice. Finally, the minimum detectable effects drawn are the AR-stage ones in panel (a) (0.18% and 0.40%) and the entity-stage one in panel (b) (0.08%): the larger, more conservative rules are used where the two differ, and both are labelled on the artefact.
