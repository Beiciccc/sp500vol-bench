This work uses only public corporate disclosures and licensed market data about publicly traded firms. No human participants were recruited and no personal data within the meaning of data-protection law were processed as research subjects: SEC filings name corporate officers in statutory disclosures that firms are legally required to publish, but the study performs no profiling or re-identification and individuals are never units of analysis. The disclosure corpus was retrieved from the SEC EDGAR full-text archive \citep{sec2024edgar} in compliance with its fair-access policy: every request carried a declared user-agent and contact address, the rate was throttled below the published request-rate ceiling with exponential back-off, and documents were cached so none was fetched twice.

Daily stock data and the CRSP/Compustat-Merged link table were obtained through an institutional Wharton Research Data Services subscription \citep{crsp2026data}, whose licence permits academic use but prohibits redistribution of the raw data. We therefore release no raw extract; instead we provide regeneration scripts and a data dictionary so that any researcher with their own WRDS credentials can rebuild the panel exactly, preserving reproducibility without breaching the licence. The artefacts reported here are derived aggregate statistics and evaluation metrics, which constitute permitted summarisation.

Financial forecasting research carries a specific hazard: selectively reported or overstated results can mislead readers into ascribing economic value that does not exist. Our headline finding is a near-null and it is reported in full---every run is documented, every day-clustered Diebold--Mariano comparison is tabulated across the 180 standalone comparisons and the 69-cell combination grid, the seed-ensemble primary was declared in advance, and no configuration was dropped after seeing its test results. The survivorship-free, point-in-time benchmark construction is itself a guard against the selection bias that silently inflates naive EDGAR-to-market joins. The models are research artefacts for studying information content, not investment advice. The compute footprint is disclosed: fine-tuning and probing consumed 590.4 GPU-hours across the C and D blocks, the single largest configuration being Longformer fine-tuning at 254.7 GPU-hours, with the prompted arms served by offline LLM inference; per-configuration GPU-hour totals and baseline CPU wall-clock times are reported so the cost of reproduction is known in advance.
